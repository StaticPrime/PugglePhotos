# PugglePhotos

**A cozy home for your photo library and the memories in it.**

PugglePhotos is a photo album for the Mac that works with the photos and videos already in your Photos library. Wander through every day you've photographed, gather favorites into albums, give photos labels, captions and the stories behind them, turn a photo over to read what's written on its back, write about your days in a journal, and put photos back on the right day just by dragging them there. Each morning it can tell you about this day in earlier years, and play those photos back as a slideshow with music. Any album opens as a photo book whose pages turn like a real one's, with its notes on sticky notes or written by the photos, and room to write anywhere on its pages.

- Memories from this day in past years, big days and trips, years and labels, each a slideshow
- A journal: a mood and a few words for any day, shown with that day's photos and in its memories
- Photo books, glued or on rings in a binder, with instant photos and prints tilted on the page, notes on sticky notes or in an instant photo's white space, writing anywhere on the pages, and videos that play right there
- Photos that turn over to show their backs, with the note written there in handwriting you choose
- A timeline of every day, with a calendar that shows where your photos are
- Albums, as many as you like, with covers and stories, and a photo can be in any number of them
- Shared albums: share an album and its book with family and friends who have PugglePhotos, and look through theirs
- Colorful labels, captions and notes on any photo
- Drag photos onto a day, or between two photos, to fix all their dates at once
- Drag photos and videos in from anywhere to add them: onto the library, Favorites, an album or a label
- Gallery, list and album covers, grouped and sorted your way

PugglePhotos reads your Photos library where it is: nothing in it is copied or moved, and photos and videos you drag in go straight into it. Albums are kept in step with your Photos library, and so are dates and favorites, so they're right everywhere you see your photos. Labels, captions, notes and your journal stay on your Mac, apart from the albums you share, which go through iCloud to the people you share them with.

PugglePhotos is free, needs no account of its own and has no tracking. It runs in the macOS app sandbox with no permission to use the network (macOS takes shared albums to and from iCloud for it), on macOS 14 Sonoma or later. It's written in Swift with AppKit and SwiftUI, and is part of the PuggleSoft family with [PuggleDocs](https://github.com/StaticPrime/PuggleDocs), [PuggleSheets](https://github.com/StaticPrime/PuggleSheets) and [PuggleSlides](https://github.com/StaticPrime/PuggleSlides).

## Features

### Browsing

- **The window.** The sidebar on the left (Memories, the Journal, your library, albums and labels, with a calendar underneath), your photos in the middle and the info panel on the right (View ▸ Show Info, ⌘I). The toolbar has Back, gallery or list (and book, in an album), a thumbnail size slider, grouping and sorting, the favorite heart and search. Light and Dark Mode both work.
- **The timeline.** Library shows every photo and video under the day it was taken ("Today", "Yesterday", "Saturday, June 14, 2025"), oldest first, opening on the newest. Each day's heading stays at the top while its photos scroll by, and counts what's under it; click a heading to select that day's photos. A day you wrote about shows your words under its name. Go ▸ Today (⌘T) jumps to the most recent day.
- **Gallery, list and grouping.** View as a gallery of thumbnails (⌘1), a list (⌘2) with each photo's title, date, kind, size, albums, labels and note. Group by day, month, year, album or label (a photo in two albums shows in both, and the ones in none come last), or not at all. Sort by date taken, date added, title or kind, either way round. Library, Favorites, Videos and Recently Added each remember their own choice, as do albums and labels. Click a list column's heading to sort by it. ⌘+ and ⌘− (or the slider) change the thumbnail size.
- **The map.** Map, under Videos in the sidebar (Go ▸ Map, ⌥⌘7), shows every photo and video that says where it was taken, small, at that spot on a map; ones that don't, like screenshots and scans, stay off it. Photos close together are gathered under one, its newest favorite (or else its newest photo) on top and how many there are in a pink badge, and they part as you zoom in. Click a group to zoom in on it, and a photo (or photos taken in one spot) to open it, then go through the photos in view with ← and →; Esc goes back to the map just where it was. Searching narrows the map to what it finds.
- **The calendar.** A month in the sidebar, with every day that has photos tinted, more strongly the more there are. Click a day to go to it; click the month to jump to any month of any year with photos; scroll over it to turn the months. From the keyboard, the arrow keys move between days, Page Up and Page Down turn the month, and Return goes to the day. View ▸ Hide Calendar puts it away.
- **The viewer.** Double-click, Return or Space opens a photo as big as the window allows, on a dark background. ← and → step through the photos, pinching, double-clicking or ⌘+ zooms in, and dragging moves around a zoomed photo. Videos play right there. A filmstrip along the bottom shows the photos around the one open, keeping it in the middle; click one to open it (View ▸ Hide Filmstrip puts it away). Esc, ⌘[ or Back returns to the photos with the same one selected.
- **Get Info.** Right-click a photo or video in the gallery, the list or the viewer and choose Get Info (or Photo ▸ Get Info, ⌥⌘I) for a little window of its own beside the library: its caption and whether it's a favorite, its file's name, when it was taken, what the file says about the camera (its make and model, the lens, ISO, focal length, exposure, aperture and shutter speed), how many pixels it has and how big the file is, its note and labels, and where it was taken, named and on a small map. Write a caption or note, add or take off labels, favorite it, choose Adjust to change when it was taken, and Change Place… to search for a place or click the map to put the pin there, or Remove Place; the date and place change in your Photos library too, and Edit ▸ Undo takes any of it back. Open as many as you like, one for each photo; each keeps up as the photo changes. A photo from an album shared with you shows its information but can't be changed.
- **Search.** The search field (⌘F) finds photos by caption, note, label, album, file name, kind ("video", "screenshot"), "favorite", year ("2019"), month ("june"), or what you wrote in the journal about their day (or its mood); every word has to match.
- **Thumbnails** show a heart for favorites, the length of videos, a dot for each label and a bubble when a photo has a note. Settings can hide all but the video length.
- **The back of a photo.** Click a photo to choose it, then click it again to turn it over: its back is photo paper with the day it was taken written in the corner, its caption, and its note. Click it again, choose another photo or press Esc to turn it back. The pencil on the back, Photo ▸ Write on the Back (⌥⌘B) or the right-click menu opens it to write on; a click elsewhere keeps what you wrote, and only Cancel throws it away.

### Memories and slideshows

- **Memories.** The Memories page (Go ▸ Memories, ⌥⌘0) starts with this day in earlier years: a card for each year ("1 Year Ago Today", "3 Years Ago Today") and one with every year together, plus "This Week, 2 Years Ago" for a year with photos from around today but not on it. Below come highlights (days with lots of photos, with days in a row joined into one trip, named for the album most of their photos are in, a holiday or a label, or else their dates), your favorites and the days you wrote about, a card for each year, and one for each label with enough photos. Screenshots are never memories. Click a card to play it; right-click for Show Photos, which lists its photos like any album.
- **Slideshows.** A memory plays full screen: a title card, then each photo with a slow pan and zoom (photos of a different shape sit whole over a blurred copy of themselves), each video playing in place with the music turned down, and each photo's date and caption; one picture crossfades into the next. A bar of segments along the top fills as each photo plays. Click the right of the screen or press → for the next photo, the left or ← for the one before; Space pauses, M quiets the music and Esc ends it. The last card offers Play Again, Show Photos and Done. View ▸ Play Slideshow (⌥⌘P), or the Play Slideshow button at the top of an album, plays an album, a memory, the selected photos, or the photos showing from the selected one on.
- **Music.** Slideshows play PugglePhotos' own gentle tune, made from scratch, or a song of your own, chosen in Settings, or nothing. Settings also sets how long each photo shows.
- **A morning notification.** On days with photos from the same day in earlier years, a notification at the time you choose (9:00 AM unless you change it) says so; click it and the slideshow plays. The next month's notifications are set ahead, so they come even when PugglePhotos isn't open.
- **Open at login.** Settings ▸ Open PugglePhotos when I log in starts it quietly at login, as a paw in the menu bar with today's memories in its menu, so notifications stay on time. Opening PugglePhotos brings back its window and Dock icon.

### Your journal

- **Writing about a day.** The pencil on any day's heading in the library opens a small page to write on: pick a mood, write a few words, and they show under the day's name. A click elsewhere keeps what you wrote; Cancel or Esc throws it away. File ▸ Write in Journal (⌘J) writes about the day of the photo you've chosen, or today, and the info panel shows what you wrote about a photo's day.
- **The Journal page.** Journal in the sidebar (Go ▸ Journal, ⌥⌘6) has a place to write about today, or about Another Day, with every entry below it, newest first, beside a few of that day's photos. An entry can be edited, played as a slideshow, opened in the library or deleted, and Undo puts back any change.
- **In your memories.** A memory of a day you wrote about quotes it on its card and on its slideshow's title card, which stays up a little longer so there's time to read it, and its notification mentions it. Days You Wrote About plays them all, and search finds a day's photos by what you wrote.

### Photo books

- **An album as a book.** Click Open Book at the top of an album, choose View ▸ as Book (⌘3), or right-click an album and choose Open as Book. The book starts closed, on a cloth cover with the album's cover photo and name; inside are endpapers, a title page with the album's name, dates and story, then the photos, one, two or three to a page, each leaning a little and a little out of line, with captions written underneath and page numbers.
- **Turning pages.** Click the right page to turn forward and the left page to turn back (while a photo, sticky note or writing is picked, a click on the paper just lets it go), press → or ←, swipe with two fingers, or drag a page over by hand and let go: past a third of the way it turns, otherwise it settles back. Pages lift and curl over the spine, and the book slides to the middle as it opens and closes. The bar under the book shows where you are ("Pages 3–4 of 12").
- **Arranging the pages.** Click a photo to pick it, then drag it anywhere on its page, drag the round knob above it to turn it (hold Shift for steps of 15°), or drag its corner to make it bigger or smaller, keeping its shape, from a small print up to a whole page (the pointer becomes a hand over any handle); [ and ] tilt it a little, Photo ▸ Straighten stands it up, Photo ▸ Original Size gives it back the size the book gave it, and Leave Out of Book takes it out of the book but not the album. Click the photo picked to turn it over and read its back; double-click a photo to see it on its own. Everything can be undone.
- **Moving photos between pages.** A photo dragged in a book lifts off its page and can be put down anywhere on either page, however many photos the book puts to a page; the photos left behind stay where they are. Hold it at the book's outer edge and the page turns under it: at the left edge back to the spread before, to put it on that spread's right page, and at the right edge forward to the next spread's left page, as many pages as it's held there. Before the first page of photos, or after the last, the page stays. Pages added to write on take photos too. Let go off the pages, a photo goes back where it was.
- **Notes in the book.** A photo's note is written in an instant photo's white space, or on the page under other kinds of photos. Notes in the bar (or View ▸ Notes) puts them all on sticky notes instead, across the photos, or hides them, and any photo can choose for itself, or hide its note, from its right-click menu.
- **Sticky notes.** Square, colored, a little lifted at one corner, with the note written on them. Drag one anywhere, even hanging off its photo; stretch it by its corner, turn it by its knob, give it another color from its right-click menu, and double-click it to write on it. It moves with its photo, and keeps its place on it when the photo is made bigger or smaller. Reset Book Layout puts sticky notes back where they started.
- **Writing on the pages.** Write in the bar (or View ▸ Write on Pages) picks up the pen: click anywhere on any page, the covers, the endpapers and the title page included, and write right there. A click elsewhere, Esc or ⌘Return finishes; double-click to write on it again. Drag writing anywhere, change how wide its lines run with the bar at its side, turn it by its knob, and give it another ink or size, or erase it, from its right-click menu or with Delete. Ink is dark on light paper and light on black pages and the cloth.
- **Pages to write on.** Add Page puts a blank page beside the open ones, numbered with the rest; Remove This Page takes an added page out again. Writing on the blank page that finishes the last sheet keeps that page.
- **Handwriting.** Everything in a book is written in its handwriting: the one chosen in Settings, or the book's own, chosen with Handwriting in the bar.
- **The look.** The bar (and View ▸ Paper, Frames, Tilt, Layout and Cover) sets the paper (cream, kraft, black album pages, blush, sky or linen), the frames (instant photos with room to write underneath, prints with a white border, plain photos, or prints held down with tape), how much the photos lean, how many share a page, the cover's cloth, and the binding. Reset Book Layout puts back every photo you moved, turned or resized. Each album keeps its own book.
- **Binding.** Binding in the bar (or View ▸ Binding) glues the pages into the cover, meeting in the middle, or holds them on three or four rings in a binder. Either way the book opens on padded boards in the cover's cloth, a little bigger than its pages, with the edges of the pages already turned, and those still to come, showing at its sides; a glued cover folds into the middle. The cover is one of those boards, closed or turning just as when it lies open: it opens and closes whole, its border past the pages showing all the way over and the endpaper pasted inside it, and a binder's spine and rings stay inside it at the binding, showing only where the cover has swung clear of them and never out beside it. A binder has the ring mechanism down its spine and the rings arching over from page to page through punched holes, each lined with an eyelet, and a page turns on the rings: they stay through its holes all the way over, running over the page from the side it lifts from and under it to the other. Closed, the cover shows its spine: a glued book's rounds over like a hardback's, its shoulder catching the light, with the joint pressed in beside it where the cover hinges and the cloth turned in at the headcaps; a binder's shows its padded edge. The rings (and eyelets) come in silver, gold, rose gold or black.
- **Videos on the page.** A video plays in its frame while the pointer is over it, with sound unless Settings says otherwise. Settings ▸ Always play the videos on the open pages plays them all, without sound, whenever their pages are open.
- **PDF, EPUB and printing.** The pink download button floating at the book's top right saves it as a PDF or an EPUB (so do File ▸ Export Book as PDF, ⇧⌘E, and Export Book as EPUB). A PDF is the book to share with anyone, PugglePhotos or not, just as it lies open in the window: a wide page for the front cover, one for each spread and one for the back cover, each the book on the desk with its cloth cover, binding, rings, page edges, sticky notes and shadow, sharp at any size, with the photos at print size and kept as JPEG, so the file stays small enough to send. An EPUB is the book as an e-book, to read in e-book apps on phones, tablets and computers: every page of the book, each drawn just as the window draws it, photos, sticky notes, writing, binding and all, the covers on their own and the pages that face each other side by side wherever the screen is wide enough, with a table of contents, the page numbers, and each page told in words for anyone listening rather than looking. File ▸ Print Book (⌘P) prints a spread to a sheet.

### Shared albums

- **Sharing an album.** File ▸ Share Album… (or Share Album… in an album's right-click menu) shares it with family and friends who have PugglePhotos; the + beside Shared Albums in the sidebar, or File ▸ New Shared Album…, makes a new album from the photos chosen and shares it. Invite Participants sends its link by Messages, Mail or AirDrop, or choose Anyone with the Link and Copy Link to pass the link on yourself; whoever clicks it joins, and the album opens in PugglePhotos. The photos and videos go at full size, with their captions and notes, the album's cover and story, and its whole book: its look and handwriting, every photo where you put it, sticky notes and writing.
- **Room in iCloud.** A shared album is kept in your iCloud storage, and the sharing sheet says how much room it takes. If your iCloud storage fills up before an album has all gone, sharing stops, what went up is taken back out, and the sheet says how much room the album needs and how much more to make. Close the sheet on an album whose sharing stopped partway for any other reason, and what went up of it is taken back out too.
- **Keeping it up to date.** A shared album stays yours to change: add photos, take them out, write captions, rearrange its book, and everyone it's shared with sees the change a moment later. If a change can't go (your iCloud storage is full, say), you're told once, its sheet says why, and it goes with everything changed meanwhile when PugglePhotos next comes to the front. Manage Shared Album… shows who's in it, invited or joined, invites more and takes people out, and Stop Sharing… takes it back from everyone, leaving the album in your library.
- **Albums shared with you** are listed under Shared Albums in the sidebar, with who shared them, to look through in the gallery, the list, the viewer and as a book, laid out just as their owner made it. They're to look at only: nothing about their photos can be changed, and their photos can't be put in your albums. They stay out of your library, its days and its memories. A notification tells you when someone shares an album with you, and when they add photos to it; click it to open the album. File ▸ Leave Shared Album… takes one off your Mac.

### Organizing

- **Adding photos and videos.** Drag them in from anywhere (the Finder, the desktop, a whole folder of them, or another app) and drop them on the window or in the sidebar, and they go into your Photos library; the files stay where they were. Where they land decides where else they go: on Library, Videos or Recently Added, just the library; on Favorites, they're favorites; on an album or a label, they join it; on Albums, they make a new album. Dropped on the photos showing, they join what's showing, and the window lights up to say where they'll go. Only photos and videos are taken, and each is dated as its camera wrote, or by its file if it says nothing. A file already in your library is noticed first: skip it (it still goes where you dropped it) or add it again. The new photos are picked out where they land, and Undo takes them out again, into Recently Deleted.
- **Albums.** File ▸ New Album (⌘N), or New Album with Selection (⇧⌘N), names the album right in the sidebar. Add photos by dragging them onto an album in the sidebar or onto its cover, from the Photo ▸ Add to Album menu or from the info panel. A photo can be in any number of albums. An album opens with its title and story at the top, both editable where they are, with its dates and size. Show it oldest or newest first, or in its own order, and drag photos around to arrange them. Delete removes photos from the album (never from your library); Photo ▸ Make Album Cover picks its cover.
- **Albums in step with your Photos library.** Every album is also an album in your Photos library. Make, rename, fill, arrange or delete one here and the same happens there; albums made or changed there, on this Mac or your other devices, show up here. Each change goes to the library as one change, and Undo puts it back in both places. An album's story and cover stay with PugglePhotos.
- **All Albums.** Every album's cover as a little stack of prints, with its dates and size, sorted by name, date, date created or size. Double-click a cover to open the album, drop photos on it to add them, or click New Album.
- **Labels.** Make labels with a name, a color and an emoji ("😂 Funny", "🐶 Pets"). Put them on photos from the info panel or the Photo ▸ Labels menu, by dropping photos on a label in the sidebar or on a label's heading, or with ⌃1 to ⌃9 for the first nine. A label in the sidebar shows every photo that has it, by month.
- **Captions and notes.** Give any photo a caption, its title in lists and the viewer, and a note for the story behind it, right in the info panel. Albums have a story of their own.
- **Favorites.** Press period (.), click the heart in the toolbar or the info panel, or drop photos on Favorites. Favorites are the same ones as in your Photos library.
- **Several at once.** Select several photos and the info panel shows how many there are and their dates, with every label marked on all, some or none of them, and buttons to favorite them, add them to an album or change their dates together.
- **Undo** (⌘Z) and **Redo** (⇧⌘Z) for every change, named for what they undo.

### Fixing dates

Scanned prints dated the day they were scanned, a camera with its clock set wrong, photos sent by a friend: PugglePhotos puts them on the right day without changing them one by one.

- **Drop photos on a day.** Drag any number of photos (Select All, ⌘A, picks every one showing) onto a day in the calendar or onto a day's heading in the timeline. Each keeps its time of day, or they all get a time you choose, a second apart in the order they were in. While dragging, hover over the calendar's arrows to turn the months.
- **Drop photos between two others.** In a view sorted by date, drop photos between two photos and they take a moment right between them, so they sit exactly there. A line in the gap shows where they'll land.
- **Change Date and Time** (⇧⌘D) sets an exact date and time for the selected photos: shift them all by the same amount (keeping the gaps between them), move them to a day keeping their times, or give them all one date and time.
- PugglePhotos asks before changing dates (Settings can turn that off), changes the whole batch in one go, then shows the photos where they landed. The new dates are saved to your Photos library, so the photos sort correctly everywhere; Edit ▸ Undo puts the old ones back.

### Accessibility

- **VoiceOver** hears each photo as what it is, when it was taken, its caption, and whether it's a favorite or has labels or a note. Every day in the calendar can be found and pressed, with its number of photos. A photo book says which pages are open, finds the photos on them and offers Next Page and Previous Page. A slideshow announces its title, what you wrote that day, and each photo as it comes.
- **The keyboard** reaches everything: each command has a menu item, most have a shortcut, and the calendar works with the arrow keys.
- **Reduce Motion** (in System Settings ▸ Accessibility ▸ Display) keeps things still: slideshow photos hold still instead of panning, book pages and photos turning over fade instead of turning, memory cards don't grow under the pointer, and the filmstrip jumps instead of sliding.

### Your library and your privacy

- The first time it opens, PugglePhotos asks for permission to use your Photos library. It reads photos where they are and never copies or moves them; it changes only what you ask it to: a photo's date, whether it's a favorite, your albums, and the photos and videos you drag in, which it adds. The only photos it ever deletes are ones you dragged in and then undid adding, and macOS asks you first.
- Labels, captions, notes, album stories and your journal are saved on your Mac in the app's own folder (`~/Library/Containers/com.pugglesoft.PugglePhotos/Data/Library/Application Support/PugglePhotos`), with a copy from each of the last 14 days kept in `Backups` next to it. A file that can't be read is set aside, never overwritten.
- PugglePhotos has no permission to use the network and sends nothing anywhere but the albums you share. Memories are found, and their notifications made, on your Mac. The map's land, roads and names come from macOS's own map service, which learns which part of the world the map shows, as any map does. Get Info asks the same service to name the place a photo was taken, which sends it that place's coordinates, and to find the places you search for, which sends the words you type; nothing else about your photos leaves your Mac.
- An album you share is kept in your iCloud storage, its photos and videos at full size, and goes from there when you stop sharing it, or if sharing it doesn't finish. Albums shared with you are kept in `Shared Albums` in the same folder as the catalog, pictures and all, until you leave them or their owner stops sharing them.

Help ▸ Keyboard Shortcuts (⌘/) lists every keyboard shortcut.

## Known limits

- macOS may ask you to confirm when PugglePhotos changes dates or favorites in your Photos library; PugglePhotos sends each batch as one change, so you're asked at most once per drop. Undoing photos you dragged in always asks, since it deletes them.
- A live photo dragged in as its picture and its movie comes in as a photo and a video. A file is noticed as already in the library by its name, size and when it was taken.
- Labels, captions, notes, album stories, covers and the journal are PugglePhotos' own and don't show in other apps. They follow photos by the library's identifiers for them, so they stay with the library on this Mac; switching to a different Photos library leaves them behind.
- Albums in folders in the Photos library show as albums of their own, and new albums are made outside any folder. Smart albums and the Photos library's own shared albums aren't shown.
- Times are shown in the Mac's current time zone.
- Places are shown as coordinates, not place names. Live photos show as still photos, and hidden photos aren't shown.
- The map shows only photos and videos that say where they were taken; a photo's place can't be set or changed in PugglePhotos. Its land and roads need an internet connection to draw, though the photos show on it without one.
- Get Info's caption is PugglePhotos' own, not the title the Photos library keeps, which macOS doesn't let other apps change; a file's name and what it says about the camera can't be changed either. Naming and finding places needs an internet connection. Info windows hide while PugglePhotos is in the background, as panels do.
- Sharing albums goes through iCloud, so it needs an iCloud account, for you and for everyone you share with, and a copy of PugglePhotos signed to use it (see Sharing through iCloud); when it can't, the sharing sheet says why. Changes reach the people an album is shared with while PugglePhotos is open, or when they next open it. A live photo is shared as its picture.
- macOS doesn't tell apps how much room is left in someone's iCloud storage, so PugglePhotos can't check before sharing that an album will fit. It says how big the album is (on macOS 27 or later, which gives the size of each photo's file), and if iCloud fills up partway, how much went up is its measure of the room there was.
- On a large library, file names arrive a little after the photos, so sorting by title settles once they have.
- Photo book pages are laid out with one to three photos each, following the album's order, so to change the order of its pages, arrange the album; any page can be given more by moving photos onto it. Books save and print the pictures at up to 1,800 pixels across, and show videos as their first frame. An EPUB's pages are pictures 1,800 by 2,340 pixels, so its words can be heard but not searched or picked out.
- Writing in a book stands straight while it's being written and takes its angle again once it's done. Writing next to photos stays with the nearest photo when the book is laid out afresh, keeping its place on the page.
- Handwriting comes from the fonts on your Mac; a book whose font is later removed is written in the one chosen in Settings.
- The journal holds one entry for each day.
- Memories and highlights are named from dates, albums, labels and a few holidays, since PugglePhotos doesn't look up place names. Slideshows play on the screen the library window is on.
- macOS asks once whether PugglePhotos may show notifications, and opening at login may need approving in System Settings ▸ General ▸ Login Items.

## Requirements

- macOS 14 Sonoma or later
- Xcode 16 or later (developed with Xcode 27.2, Swift 6)

## Building and running

Open `PugglePhotos.xcodeproj` in Xcode and run the **PugglePhotos** scheme. Or, from the command line:

```sh
xcodebuild -project PugglePhotos.xcodeproj -scheme PugglePhotos -configuration Debug \
  -derivedDataPath build/DerivedData build
open build/DerivedData/Build/Products/Debug/PugglePhotos.app
```

The app is sandboxed, with access to the Photos library. Debug builds are signed to run on the Mac that built them, unless `Config/Signing.xcconfig` names a team (see Sharing from Xcode below); releases are signed by `Tools/make-installer.sh`.

### Installer

```sh
Tools/make-installer.sh
```

This builds `build/PugglePhotos.pkg`, which installs PugglePhotos.app in /Applications for all users (macOS asks for an administrator's password). The app runs on every Mac with macOS 14 or later. Beside it goes `build/PugglePhotos.dSYM.zip`, the build's debug symbols (see Reading a crash report below).

A local build is ad-hoc signed and the installer is unsigned. That's fine on the Mac that built it, but a copy that arrives by download or email is blocked the first time: the user has to click **Open Anyway** in System Settings ▸ Privacy & Security. To sign with a Developer ID and have the installer notarized, set the variables described at the top of `Tools/make-installer.sh` before running it. Releases built on GitHub are signed and notarized this way when the repository has the Developer ID secrets (see below).

### Releasing a new version

Versions are MAJOR.MINOR.FIX. To release the next one, run this on `main`, up to date with GitHub and with everything committed:

```sh
Tools/release.sh            # a fix:          1.2.3 → 1.2.4
Tools/release.sh minor      # a minor update: 1.2.3 → 1.3.0
Tools/release.sh major      # a major update: 1.2.3 → 2.0.0
```

The script raises the version and the build number in the Xcode project, commits "Release version 1.2.4", tags it `v1.2.4` and pushes both. The tag starts the Release workflow on GitHub, which runs the package tests, builds the installer with `Tools/make-installer.sh` and publishes it, with its debug symbols, as a GitHub release. The installer keeps the same name in every release, so https://github.com/StaticPrime/PugglePhotos/releases/latest/download/PugglePhotos.pkg always downloads the newest one. `Tools/release.sh --dry-run` shows the next version without changing anything.

To sign and notarize releases, add these secrets under the repository's Settings ▸ Secrets and variables ▸ Actions. Without them, releases are published unsigned, with a warning on the run.

| Secret | What it holds |
| --- | --- |
| `DEVELOPER_ID_P12_BASE64` | A .p12 file with the "Developer ID Application" and "Developer ID Installer" certificates and their private keys, base64-encoded (`base64 -i DeveloperID.p12 \| pbcopy`) |
| `DEVELOPER_ID_P12_PASSWORD` | The .p12 file's password |
| `NOTARY_API_KEY` | The text of an App Store Connect API key file (`AuthKey_….p8`), used to notarize |
| `NOTARY_KEY_ID` | That key's ID |
| `NOTARY_ISSUER_ID` | The issuer ID shown above the keys in App Store Connect |
| `DEVELOPER_ID_PROFILE_BASE64` | Optional: the Developer ID provisioning profile for iCloud, base64-encoded (see below). With it, released copies can share albums |

### Reading a crash report

Release builds are stripped, so a crash report from an installed copy shows the PugglePhotos lines of its stack only as addresses. Every release after 1.0.0 carries `PugglePhotos.dSYM.zip`, the debug symbols for that build. Unzip the one for the version in the report and look the addresses up, giving the load address from the PugglePhotos line under Binary Images:

```sh
atos -o PugglePhotos.app.dSYM/Contents/Resources/DWARF/PugglePhotos -arch arm64 -l 0x102048000 0x1020b5e4c 0x1020d2c2c
```

Use `-arch x86_64` for a report from an Intel Mac. Symbols fit only the build they came with: `dwarfdump --uuid PugglePhotos.app.dSYM` should list the UUID the report gives for PugglePhotos.

### Sharing through iCloud

Shared albums are kept in iCloud, in the container `iCloud.com.pugglesoft.PugglePhotos`, so only a copy of PugglePhotos signed for that container can share them. Any other copy, like one built from a fresh clone, says so in the sharing sheet; on the sample library, debug runs share through a stand-in for iCloud on the Mac instead. To set it up, once:

1. In the Apple Developer account, under Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles ▸ Identifiers, register the iCloud container `iCloud.com.pugglesoft.PugglePhotos`. Then edit the App ID `com.pugglesoft.PugglePhotos`: turn on iCloud, with CloudKit and that container, and Push Notifications.
2. Under Profiles, make a Developer ID provisioning profile for that App ID and the Developer ID Application certificate, and download it.
3. Make the container's schema. iCloud's development environment adds record types as they're first saved, but production only takes what's deployed to it. So share an album once from Xcode in the development environment (see Sharing from Xcode below), which adds `Album` (a Bytes field `info`), `Item` (a Bytes field `info` and an Asset field `original`) and iCloud's own record of a share, `cloudkit.share`. Then deploy the schema to production in CloudKit Console.
4. Add the profile to the repository as the secret `DEVELOPER_ID_PROFILE_BASE64` (`base64 -i PugglePhotos.provisionprofile | pbcopy`). To build a signed installer on your Mac instead, set `DEVELOPER_ID_PROFILE` to the profile's path along with the Developer ID variables before running `Tools/make-installer.sh`.

`Tools/make-installer.sh` then puts the profile inside the app and signs it for the container, the production environment and pushes. To try sharing, install it on two Macs signed in to different iCloud accounts.

#### Sharing from Xcode

A debug build can share albums too, once it's signed with the team. Make `Config/Signing.xcconfig` (Git ignores it) with your team's ID:

```
DEVELOPMENT_TEAM = ABCDE12345
CODE_SIGN_IDENTITY = Apple Development
CODE_SIGN_ENTITLEMENTS = Config/iCloud.entitlements
```

Add an Apple ID in that team under Xcode ▸ Settings ▸ Accounts, then build: Xcode makes the development certificate and provisioning profile itself. Set the team in that file rather than under *Signing & Capabilities*, which would write it into the project for everyone. Debug builds use iCloud's production environment, as installed copies do, so an album shared from Xcode reaches someone who installed PugglePhotos. Production only takes the kinds of records already deployed to it: when PugglePhotos starts saving a new kind, and once at the start, add `ICLOUD_ENVIRONMENT = Development` to `Config/Signing.xcconfig`, share an album, deploy the schema to production in CloudKit Console, and take the line out again. `Config/iCloud.entitlements` is `PugglePhotos/PugglePhotos.entitlements` with iCloud's added, so keep the two alike.

## How it works

- **The photos** stay in the Photos library. PugglePhotos reads every item's date, size, kind and favorite through PhotoKit off the main thread, asks for thumbnails at the size each view shows them (and keeps them in memory by size, with the ones a screen above and below got ready ahead while scrolling), and follows changes made elsewhere as they happen.
- **The catalog** is everything made in PugglePhotos: albums with their photos in order, labels, captions, notes and journal entries (by day), all referring to photos by the library's identifiers. It's one small JSON file, written whole and atomically half a second after each change.
- **Albums are mirrored.** Each album is linked to its twin in the Photos library, which decides the album's title and photos whenever the two differ: each time the library changes, its albums are read again and new, changed and deleted ones are taken in. A change made in PugglePhotos shows at once and goes to the twin as one change, which compares first so only what differs is changed; changes made while one is on its way follow it together. If the library refuses, it keeps its version and so does PugglePhotos.
- **Days** are worked out once per item as plain day numbers, so grouping a large library by day, month or year is arithmetic rather than calendar lookups. The grid's layout places thumbnails by arithmetic too, section by section, and asks only for what's on screen, which keeps scrolling smooth with tens of thousands of photos.
- **Adding files** reads each one dropped off the main thread (ImageIO for a photo's size and the date its camera wrote, AVFoundation for a video's), looking through folders all the way down; files another app only promises, like attachments and pictures from web pages, are written out to a temporary folder first. They go to the Photos library twenty at a time, each batch one change; a batch the library refuses is tried again a file at a time, so the rest still go in and the refused ones can be named. A file with no date inside is given the earliest date it has on disk. The whole drop, from adding the files to putting them in their album, label or Favorites, is one step to undo.
- **Dragging photos** puts one item per photo on the drag. The sidebar, the calendar, the headings and the gallery only check that a drag holds photos while it passes over them, and read which ones when it's dropped, so a whole library dragged at once stays smooth. The gallery shows the line where photos would land between others, and none over a day whose photos are all being dragged, since there's nothing left there to land beside.
- **Moving photos in time** works out every new date first, keeping the photos in the order they had, then sends the whole batch to the Photos library as one change. Undo sends the old dates back the same way.
- **Memories** come from an index of photos by calendar day, built once per change to the library, away from the main thread. A memory's photos are chosen by score (favorites, captions, notes, labels) with the rest spread across its time, and bursts of near-identical shots count once.
- **Slideshows** are layers on the screen: each slide's photo moves from one part of it to another with Core Animation (the same seed always gives the same motion), and slides crossfade. Pausing finishes the crossfade and holds the motion where it is. Pictures for the next slides are fetched ahead at the screen's size.
- **Photo books** are laid out by the package from the album's photos and the book's settings: each page picks one, two or three photos (a seeded pattern for "A Mix"), places their frames in spaces chosen by their shapes, then nudges and leans each by an amount that comes from the photo and the seed, so a book is the same every time. Moves, turns and sizes made by hand are kept per photo and laid over the result, and so is a page a photo was carried to, found by the first photo the book puts there (so it's the same page however the book is laid out) or by an added page's name; a photo resized by its corner keeps its shape and its opposite corner where it was. One renderer draws a page with Core Graphics for the screen, the page turns, PDFs and printing; a turn is a two-sided leaf rotating in 3D about the spine (a binder's rings, the page held half the spine's width off them), turned through its angle so it always lifts toward the eye, with a page picture on each side; a cover's leaf is its whole board, its own picture outside and the endpaper laid on the board inside, and a closed book shows that same board, in the window and on a PDF page alike. The cover's boards and the stacked page edges (and a binder's spine) are drawn under the pages and a binder's rings over them, again only when the book's look or size changes. While a page turns on rings, two copies of them are laid in depth, from the holes of the pages lying either side to the turning page's, keyframed with the page's own timing. The leaf and the rings are seen in a perspective of their own and laid flat over the pages: the book sorts what's in it by depth, and the tipped rings would otherwise cut across the flat layers and muddle which goes on top. Where everything lies on the desk (the pages, boards, spine, rings and shadow) is worked out once for the window and for a PDF page, and a PDF page draws the same art and pages itself, sharp, instead of laying out pictures of them; its shadow is blurred twice as far, which is what matches Core Animation's. Quartz writes a gradient into a PDF without its colors' transparency, so a see-through gradient is drawn as a picture of itself, which keeps it, on screen and on paper alike. Core Graphics offsets and blurs shadows in pixels whatever the drawing's scale, so the renderer gives them in page units and scales them to the drawing: they fall the same on the page at any size and on any screen. An EPUB's pages are pictures of the pages by the same renderer, three pixels to a point, packed as JPEG into a ZIP archive the package writes itself (the file naming what kind it is stored first, as e-book readers look for it), each page a fixed size and set beside the page it faces, as in the book.
- **Shared albums** are each one value: the photos and videos in the album's order, every one under a name of its own that's the same on each Mac it's shared to and with the seed its place in the book comes from, so it lies on the page as it does in its owner's book; their captions and notes; the cover, story and book, with every photo in it renamed to those shared names. A moment after the library or catalog changes, what's shared of each album of yours is worked out again and sent if it differs, with files only for photos not sent before, so a run of changes goes as one. In iCloud, each album you share is a zone of its own in your private database, shared whole: a record for each photo or video, holding what's said about it as JSON and its original file, and one for the album, saying which photos are in it and in what order. The album's record is saved last, so the people it's shared with see a photo only once it has all come. Albums shared with you are read from your shared database whenever iCloud says they've changed (by a push while PugglePhotos is open, and when it opens, comes to the front or has waited 15 minutes), downloading only the pictures not yet on the Mac. They're kept in a folder of their own, with their pictures, apart from the catalog. Their photos join the library's look-ups but not its photos, days or counts, under names no library photo can have, so the catalog won't take them into albums or give them labels, and they never mix with yours.
- **The map** is MapKit's, with each cluster of photos laid on it as an annotation of PugglePhotos' own. The package works out where each photo lies on the map's square of the world and gathers the ones in view (and a margin around it) by the square of a grid they fall in, the grid's squares a power of two across for the scale the map's shown at: photos regroup only when it's zoomed a whole step in or out, and stay put as it's moved about. Clusters that would still sit almost on top of each other are joined, the fullest keeping its place. Only the clusters that change are taken off the map or put on it. Gathering 60,000 photos takes a couple of milliseconds.
- **Get Info** reads a photo's file through a copy PhotoKit writes out for it (fetching it from iCloud if need be), which ImageIO reads for the camera and how it was set, and a video's through AVFoundation; the package puts what it finds into the words cameras use. Places are named and found by MapKit's geocoder and local search (on the sample library, by a stand-in that knows its places, so nothing leaves the Mac). A new date or place goes to the Photos library as one change, with its undo registered at once like every other change.
- **Notes and writing in books** are kept with the book. A sticky note's place, size, lean and color are kept in its photo's own coordinates, so it moves and turns with the photo. Writing remembers its page by what the page is (a cover, an endpaper, the title page), by the photo nearest it on a page of photos, or by an added page's own name, so it finds its page again however the book is laid out. Writing is drawn in place above the page while it's being written, and the page leaves it off meanwhile.
- **Turning a photo over** turns a two-sided card in 3D: the photo on the front and its back on the reverse, the back drawn in the chosen handwriting. In a book, the photo lifts off its page and turns above it, and the page leaves it off until it lands.
- **The tune** is synthesized, not recorded: eight bars of soft chords, a low note and a music-box melody in C major, with a gentle echo, rendered once into a seamless loop.
- **Notifications** for the next 30 days are worked out from the memory index and set ahead with the system, with a picture of each day's best photo, and set again whenever the library, the day or the settings change.
- **Edits** to the catalog go through one method that keeps the catalog as it was, so undo and redo restore exactly what was there. Changes to the Photos library register their undo at once and write the old values back when undone.
